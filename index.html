Origins · Ethiopia
850 AD

The goatherd and the dancing goats

Legend says a goatherd named Kaldi noticed his goats dancing after eating red berries from a wild shrub in the Ethiopian highlands. Those berries were coffee cherries.

FactArabica grows wild in the forests of southwest Ethiopia. It is the birthplace of the species we drink most.
Yemen · The first cups
1450

Brewed to pray through the night

The first written record of coffee as a drink comes from Yemen, across the Red Sea from Ethiopia. Sufi monks drank it to stay awake through night prayers.

HowThey roasted the beans in a pan over open fire, then ground and brewed them.
MochaYemen’s port of Mocha became the world’s coffee market for centuries. The name stuck.
Mecca · The coffee trials
1511

“Burn it all”

Mecca’s governor, Khair Beg, had a council of scholars declare coffee harmful. Coffeehouses were shut and beans burned in the streets.

WhyCoffeehouses were spreading fast, and people met there to talk politics.
ThenThe Sultan in Cairo, a coffee lover, overruled the ban within months.
MYTH VS FACT · HOW ROASTING BEGAN

MYTHA monk threw “the devil’s berries” into the fire. They smelled so good they were raked out and brewed: the first roast.

FACTSufis in Yemen were already roasting beans in a pan by the 1400s.

Who grows our coffee

Four regions to know

Vietnam is the world’s number two, almost all robusta for commercial coffee.

BrazilNo. 1 producer · ~38% of the world

The biggest producer and exporter. Low-altitude plateaus and machine harvesting. Nutty, chocolatey, low acidity.

Ethiopia & KenyaEast Africa

Ethiopia is coffee’s birthplace, with thousands of wild varieties: floral and fruity. Kenya is famous for bright, blackcurrant acidity.

PanamaBoquete · Home of Geisha

A small producer with a big name. Geisha from Boquete’s misty highlands is floral and tea-like, and has set world auction records.

Yunnan, ChinaThe new frontier

Grows about 98% of China’s coffee. Large-scale farming only began in 1988, and is growing as China drinks more coffee and wants its own supply.

Hot, hardy lowlands

GrowsHot lowlands, sea level to 800 m. Hardy and high-yield.
TasteBold, bitter, nutty and earthy, with thick crema.
CaffeineAbout twice arabica

Giant trees in the humid tropics
